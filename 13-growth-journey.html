Is the growth real? What this data says

  • YouTube is the strongest evidence. Public counts are harder to fake, and 11 of 12 videos have views of 1.3x to 7x her 51.8K subscribers, which means people outside her base are finding them.
  • Instagram likes are 0.27% to 1.85% of followers. That is low on the promo reels and healthy on the movie reel. Big accounts usually have lower rates than small ones, so this is not alarming, but it suggests many followers came from viral spikes and do not engage with every post.
  • Like counts vary a lot (3K to 20K). Bought engagement tends to look flat; this looks like real posts that perform differently.
  • Comment counts are inflated by the keyword call to action, so they say little about sentiment either way. Blank-avatar keyword commenters are normal in these funnels.
  • The totals add up. The 12 long videos (about 1.63M) plus 12 Shorts (about 41K) fit inside the channel total of 1.76M views, leaving about 90K for her other videos. Numbers that reconcile this way are a good sign.
  • Play counts and likes line up. Likes are about 3-6% of plays on her newest reels, a normal range for real viewers. Her spoken claims also check out: she said 11M for the portal reel (now 28M), nearly 8M for Spider-Verse (now 10.1M), and 800K for the iPhone ad (an Apple-store reel now shows 1.1M).
  • Verdict: the evidence now points strongly to organic growth driven by a few huge reels. Still missing: her follower curve over time.

6

What appears to have driven growth

  • A few huge hits. Four or five reels at 6-11M views would explain most of the followers (inference).
  • She is the hero. Character sheets keep her face and outfit consistent, so viewers follow a person, not random clips.
  • Familiar reference plus impossible visual. A door to a fantasy world, a Spider-Verse jump, paintings coming alive, an Apple chase scene.
  • Trend timing. The Odyssey, Spider-Man hype, the iPhone 18 launch, Apple's new CEO: she used what was already being searched.
  • Copyable formats. Hundreds of recreations of one video are free distribution.
  • Two-layer funnel. Viral reel on Instagram, full breakdown on YouTube, free guide with prompts: viewers become followers, subscribers and list members.
  • Platform partner. Every tutorial runs in Higgsfield with links; this is likely a sponsorship or affiliate relationship (not confirmed), which adds reach and money.
  • Brand-spec series. Unasked ads for Apple and Red Bull attract brand attention and a portfolio.
  • High cadence. About one post a day.
  • A clear identity. Musician and engineer, stated in the first post and bio.
  • Comment-a-keyword call to action. All four newest posts ask for ZOMBIE, SOFT, MOVIE or SAVE to get prompts or a guide. This likely feeds comments to the algorithm and probably auto-sends a link (my inference).
  • Entertainment beats promo. The pure story reel got about 4 to 7 times the likes of the tutorial promos; the promos exist to move viewers to YouTube.
  • Pinned hits. Her three biggest reels (50.7M views) are pinned at the top of the grid, so every new visitor sees proof first.
  • Recurring characters and series. Bob the cat (Kitty and Bob 3.6M, Drifting with Bob, Kaye and Bob ep.3), My World ep.3, Kaye's world 2.2M, My Robot Bestfriend 1.7M.
  • Title cards. Nearly every reel has a short, stylised title on screen (Raindance, Runaway, Falling, Slow Down), which makes the grid look like a catalogue.
  • Music-led reels. Raindance 1.6M, Runaway 761K and the Genjutsu split-screen demos (1.5M to 1.7M) work like AI music videos, directly relevant to your plan.
  • Talk reels do worst. Opinion and tip reels sit at about 100K or less, tutorial promos in the middle, cinematic ideas on top.
Cannot see: per-reel views, follower curve, paid promotion, shoutouts, or which posts flopped. Without those, the list above is a hypothesis, not proof.
7

Her repeatable content formula

Five-step loop
  1. Pick a trending cultural moment or famous scene.
  2. Add one impossible twist with her as the lead.
  3. Lock assets (character, location, prop sheets); draft at 480p; upscale winners.
  4. Post a 10-30 second vertical reel; invite recreations and ask which scene next.
  5. Publish the breakdown on YouTube with a free guide; repeat.

How to study her properly (30 minutes)

  • Sort her reels by views and list the top 10 with date, length, hook text and audio.
  • Note the first 2 seconds of each: text on screen, face, motion.
  • Mark which hits used a trend, a brand, a movie or an original idea.
  • Compare the same dates with her follower jumps if you can see them in a tracker.
  • Save the posts that started recreations and note how she invited them.
  • Repeat monthly and add the new videos to this guide.
9

What to copy from the new data

  • Make mostly entertainment reels (story, face, cinematic idea) and use a smaller number of promo reels for your link in bio.
  • Add a keyword call to action to each reel and deliver something useful (a prompt, a track preview) to build a list you own.
  • Check Instagram's automation and AI-label rules before you automate replies.
  • Track likes per follower and shares per reel from day one so you can copy what works.
  • Instagram Reels is the proven engine in her data (1.1M followers against 51.8K on YouTube). Her talking-head YouTube Shorts are weak at about 3.5K views, so test cinematic Shorts on your own before relying on them.
  • Pin your three best reels and update the pins as new hits arrive.
  • Create one recurring character and name the series (a cat, a robot, you), with a short title card on every reel.
  • Make music-led AI reels with your own tracks; they are her second-biggest category and fit your goal.

11

Risks and honest limits

  • Followers are not listeners. Put your own music under every video from the start so the audience already knows your sound.
  • Brand and movie IP. Ads with Apple, Red Bull or Spider-Man characters can be taken down; original ideas are safer for a long-term account.
  • AI backlash. Her classical-art video drew complaints; label AI clearly.
  • Credit cost. Follow the draft-first rules in this guide.
  • Time. A cloud security job leaves limited hours; batch.
